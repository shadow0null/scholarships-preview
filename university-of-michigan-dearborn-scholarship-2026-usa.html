About the scholarship

The University of Michigan-Dearborn, part of the University of Michigan system in the Detroit metropolitan area, offers merit scholarships that reduce tuition for non-resident students, including international students. The central award for this article is the undergraduate non-resident scholarship for incoming first-year students: a merit ladder published in the university’s 2026-2027 scholarship information and corroborated by several independent guides. Your award is set by the GPA on your record at the time of admission, not by a later application or interview, and it is reviewed automatically when you apply — which is why the admission deadline effectively is the scholarship deadline.

Two neighbouring awards are often confused with this one, so keep them separate. The Great Lakes Scholarship gives eligible non-resident students from Illinois, Indiana, Minnesota, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Wisconsin or Ontario (Canada) in-state tuition — it is a residency-based award, not available to most international students outside that region. The tuition-differential scholarship covers Michigan residents initially classified as out-of-state. Neither is the international merit ladder in this article, and neither should be quoted as its value.

Benefits

  • GPA 4.00 or higher: $12,500 a year, $50,000 over four years — the only route to the $50,000 total some aggregator pages quote for everyone.
  • GPA 3.50-3.99: $10,000 a year, $40,000 over four years — the figure most aggregator pages present as the single award.
  • GPA 3.25-3.49: $7,500 a year, $30,000 over four years.
  • GPA 3.00-3.24: $5,000 a year, $20,000 over four years — the entry tier, and the reason a GPA just below 3.25 or 3.50 is worth real money to improve before applying.
  • Renewal: renewable for up to eight semesters for freshmen or six semesters for transfer students, or until bachelor’s degree completion, provided you maintain at least a 3.0 cumulative GPA and complete 24 credit hours per academic year (fall and winter semesters).
  • Automatic consideration: for the listed undergraduate merit awards, the university states that students do not need a separate scholarship application — eligible applicants are automatically reviewed on residency and academic information supplied during admission.

Who can apply

  • First-year or transfer undergraduates: international or out-of-state students admitted to a full-time, degree-seeking undergraduate programme at UM-Dearborn.
  • GPA of 3.0 or higher on a 4.0 scale at admission — the minimum for the ladder, with the award level set by the exact band above. The GPA used is the one at the time of admission.
  • English proficiency: the older international criteria list IELTS 6.5, TOEFL iBT 80 (CBT 213, PBT 550) or MELAB 76. The university now operates a test-optional policy for most undergraduate courses, so confirm the current English and test requirements for your programme on the university’s admissions pages rather than treating the older list as the only route.
  • Test scores where submitted: the older international criteria also list a combined SAT score of at least 1020 (or ACT composite 20) for freshmen, and a GPA of 3.5 for transfer students, with fewer than 24 transfer credits also requiring the SAT/ACT and high-school transcripts. Under test-optional admissions these scores are not the universal gate they once were — confirm before assuming you must sit a test.
  • Full-time enrolment and progress: maintain a 3.0 cumulative GPA and 24 credits a year to renew. Changes in residency status may affect eligibility.

Documents required

  • The undergraduate admission application itself, with accurate permanent-address and residency information — this is the document the automatic scholarship review runs on, so errors here cost money.
  • Official academic transcripts (high-school transcripts for freshmen, and college transcripts for transfer students) showing the GPA the award band is set from.
  • English proficiency evidence where required for your programme (IELTS, TOEFL or an accepted equivalent).
  • SAT or ACT scores where submitted under the older criteria or where your programme still asks for them, plus any programme-specific documents the admissions pages list.
  • Some guides also list a recommendation letter, personal statement and financial support letter for the wider international application; the merit award itself is automatic on admission, so treat those as admission documents, not a separate scholarship file.

How to apply

  1. Apply for admission to an undergraduate programme at the University of Michigan-Dearborn as a first-year or transfer student, through the university’s application portal, before 15 November 2026 — the reported deadline for most international merit-based undergraduate scholarships.
  2. Supply complete transcripts and residency information, and respond promptly if the university asks for additional documentation to verify eligibility.
  3. There is no separate scholarship application for the listed merit awards: eligible applicants are automatically reviewed. Do not wait for a scholarship form that does not exist.
  4. Check your offer and award letter for the exact tier (your GPA band) and the renewal terms, and confirm the current test and English requirements for your programme before making financial commitments.

FAQs

What is the deadline for the University of Michigan-Dearborn Scholarship 2026?

15 November 2026, the date reported by multiple guides — and confirmed in substance by a study-abroad guide — as the last date for most international merit-based undergraduate scholarships. Complete your admission application by this date, since the merit review runs on admission.

How much is the scholarship worth?

It depends on your GPA at admission: $5,000 a year at GPA 3.00-3.24, $7,500 at 3.25-3.49, $10,000 at 3.50-3.99, and $12,500 at 4.00 or higher. Over four years that is $20,000, $30,000, $40,000 or $50,000 respectively. There is no flat $10,000 or flat $50,000 award for everyone.

Is there a separate scholarship application?

No, for the listed undergraduate merit awards. Eligible applicants are automatically reviewed on the residency and academic information in their admission application.

Do I need SAT or ACT scores?

The older international criteria list SAT 1020 or ACT 20, but the university now operates a test-optional policy for most undergraduate courses. Confirm the current requirement for your programme rather than assuming a test is mandatory.

What English score do I need?

The older international criteria list IELTS 6.5, TOEFL iBT 80, or MELAB 76. Confirm the current English requirement for your programme on the university’s admissions pages, since requirements vary by course.

How do I keep the scholarship?

Maintain at least a 3.0 cumulative GPA and complete 24 credit hours per academic year. The award renews for up to eight semesters for freshmen or six for transfer students, or until you complete your bachelor’s degree.

Does the Great Lakes Scholarship apply to international students?

Generally no. The Great Lakes Scholarship gives in-state tuition to non-residents from listed neighbouring states and Ontario, Canada. It is a separate, residency-based award from the international merit ladder in this article.
