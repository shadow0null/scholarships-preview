About the scholarship

The main funding route for international students at the University of Pisa is the DSU Toscana Scholarship, a regional need-based award run by DSU Toscana — the Azienda Regionale per il Diritto allo Studio Universitario della Toscana, the regional right-to-study authority serving the universities of Pisa, Florence and Siena and other Tuscan institutions. The scholarship is based primarily on the student’s economic situation and academic/merit requirements, and it can combine a monetary allowance with free meals, free accommodation for eligible off-site winners, accommodation support, exemption from the regional university study tax and full exemption from the university’s tuition fee (maximum €2,900 all-inclusive for regular students in 2026/27).

The important thing to understand is that the DSU scholarship is not a single fixed-value award that automatically covers every expense. The monetary component varies with economic circumstances and with whether the student is classified as on-site, commuter or off-site, and the exact benefits depend on the annual call. It is also a separate process from admission: you apply for your University of Pisa degree programme through the university, complete Universitaly pre-enrolment if you are a non-EU student requiring a visa, and apply for DSU through the DSU portal — three different steps, with three different deadlines.

Eligibility

  • Students pursuing their first higher-education qualification at a participating Tuscan institution, including Bachelor’s, Master’s, doctoral and eligible specialisation programmes.
  • Economic and merit criteria as set in the annual DSU call — the scholarship is need-based first, with academic requirements attached.
  • All nationalities — Italian, EU and non-EU students, including stateless students, refugees and asylum seekers, subject to the documentation rules.
  • International students with income or assets outside Italy must provide foreign financial documentation covering family composition, income, real estate and movable assets, translated into Italian and legalised as required.
  • Students from countries on DSU’s list of particularly poor and developing countries can instead submit a certificate that the family is not wealthy (per the call’s Appendix C).
  • For the PhD and specialisation window open now: students enrolled in, or intending to enrol in, eligible doctoral or specialisation programmes at the University of Pisa.

Benefits

  • Monetary scholarship — the amount varies according to economic status and student classification (on-site, commuter or off-site).
  • Free meals through DSU food services, for scholarship winners and eligible non-winners alike.
  • Free accommodation for eligible off-site scholarship winners, subject to availability.
  • Accommodation-related support for eligible students who do not receive a residence place.
  • Exemption from the regional university study tax.
  • Full exemption from the annual all-inclusive tuition fee (maximum €2,900 for regular students in 2026/27) under the University’s rules.

Documents to prepare

  • Documentation of family status — the composition of the family unit whose income and assets are considered under the DSU rules.
  • Documentation of income — official certificates showing the relevant annual income of family members.
  • Documentation of real estate / buildings — houses, land or other property owned by the applicant or family members.
  • Documentation of movable assets — bank accounts, deposits, securities and other financial holdings.
  • Foreign documents must be translated into Italian and legalised or apostilled as required — the correct route depends on the issuing country and the Apostille Convention status; check the DSU instructions and the Italian embassy or consulate for your country.
  • A Single Substitutive Declaration (DSU/ISEE) for subsidised benefits for the right to education — the call summary states it must be signed by 7 September 2026 for the general competition; check the current call for the PhD/specialisation position on this document.
  • Do not begin with a generic legalisation procedure copied from another student’s experience — the exact procedure depends on your country, the document type and the issuing authority.

How to apply

  1. Read the official DSU Toscana Scholarship & Accommodation Call 2026/27 before submitting — it sets the economic and merit criteria and the documentation rules.
  2. Prepare your financial documentation early: family status, income, property and movable assets, with translation into Italian and legalisation/apostille as required. This is the step that causes most delays for international applicants.
  3. Register on the DSU Toscana Sportello Studente portal and fill out the online application form.
  4. For PhD and specialisation courses, submit between 25 September and 16 November 2026, 1:00 PM Italian time.
  5. Separately, complete your University of Pisa admission application for your chosen programme, and Universitaly pre-enrolment if you are a non-EU student requiring a visa — these are not covered by the DSU deadline.
  6. Watch for the rankings: provisional ranking for PhD/specialisation on 15 December 2026, final ranking on 15 January 2027.

FAQs

What is the deadline for the University of Pisa DSU Scholarship 2027?

For PhD and specialisation courses: 16 November 2026 at 1:00 PM Italian time (applications open 25 September 2026). For Bachelor’s, Master’s and single-cycle degree courses the 2026/27 deadline was 7 September 2026 at 1:00 PM and has passed.

How much is the DSU Toscana Scholarship worth?

There is no single fixed amount. The monetary component varies according to the student’s economic circumstances and whether they are classified as on-site, commuter or off-site, on top of free meals, possible free accommodation, accommodation support, exemption from the regional study tax and full tuition exemption. Guides do not publish a single 2026/27 figure, so none is stated here.

Who can apply for the DSU Toscana Scholarship at the University of Pisa?

Students pursuing their first higher-education qualification at the University of Pisa (and other Tuscan institutions) — Bachelor’s, Master’s, doctoral and eligible specialisation programmes — of any nationality, subject to the economic and merit criteria in the annual DSU call. International students must provide foreign financial documentation, translated into Italian and legalised.

Is the DSU Toscana Scholarship the same as University of Pisa admission?

No. DSU Toscana is a separate regional authority, and the DSU application is a separate process through the Sportello Studente portal. You must also apply for admission to your University of Pisa programme, and complete Universitaly pre-enrolment if you are a non-EU student requiring a visa.

What documents do international students need for the DSU Scholarship?

Documentation of family status, income, real estate/buildings and movable assets (bank accounts, deposits, securities), translated into Italian and legalised or apostilled as required, plus a Single Substitutive Declaration (DSU/ISEE) for subsidised benefits. Students from countries on DSU’s list of particularly poor and developing countries can instead submit a certificate that the family is not wealthy.

When are the DSU Toscana results announced?

For PhD and specialisation courses: provisional ranking 15 December 2026, final ranking 15 January 2027. For Bachelor’s, Master’s and single-cycle courses, the provisional ranking was 30 September 2026 and the final ranking is 30 November 2026 (4 November 2026 for first-year Bachelor’s/single-cycle scholarship rankings).

What other scholarships are available at the University of Pisa?

The university also lists Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAECI) scholarships and Invest Your Talent in Italy, offered by MAECI and the university — each has its own call and deadline. Invest Your Talent in Italy for 2026/27 closed on 11 May 2026.

Do I need to know Italian to apply for the DSU Scholarship?

The application and documentation are in Italian — foreign financial documents must be translated into Italian. The DSU call and the University of Pisa’s international pages explain the requirements; check the current call for the language rules that apply to your category.
