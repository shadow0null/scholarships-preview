About the internship

The European Space Agency (ESA) runs an annual Student Internship programme that places university students inside real ESA teams — engineering, science, Earth observation, telecommunications, operations, and the corporate functions that keep a space agency flying. Internships run three to six months and are on site at ESA establishments, so this is a genuine placement inside Europe’s space program, not a remote project.

On money, the wording matters: ESA officially describes the internships as “unpaid”, but every intern receives a monthly allowance — €800 for non-residents (students living more than 50 km from the establishment, plus students with special needs regardless of where they live) and €500 for residents. Interns arrange and pay for their own health insurance, accident insurance, travel and accommodation, so budget the allowance against the cost of living near the establishment.

Eligibility

  • Be a student, preferably in the final or second-to-last year of a master’s-level university course.
  • Hold student status and stay enrolled at your university for the entire duration of the internship.
  • Be a citizen of an ESA Member State, an Associate Member, a European Cooperating State, or Canada as a Cooperating State — the programme is not open to all nationalities.
  • PhD students should not apply — ESA directs them to the Research Fellowship programme instead.
  • Respect the limits: one ESA internship per person, ever, and a maximum of two applications per call — submit more than two and all of them are disregarded.

Benefits

  • €800/month allowance for non-residents (over 50 km from the establishment; also students with special needs) or €500/month for residents.
  • Three to six months on site at an ESA establishment, working inside a real mission or corporate team.
  • A placement inside Europe’s space program — engineering, science, Earth observation, operations and more.
  • Note the intern’s own costs: health insurance, accident insurance, travel and accommodation are on you.

Documents required

  • CV and motivation letter — both required to create your candidate profile on the ESA recruitment site.
  • Your answers to the application-form questions for each opportunity you apply to.
  • No other required documents are published in the official notification.

  5. Ask ESA directly if unsureInternship questions go to ESA.Student.Internships@esa.int.

FAQs

Is the internship open to all nationalities?

No. You must be a citizen of an ESA Member State, an Associate Member, a European Cooperating State, or Canada as a Cooperating State. Some aggregator sites wrongly claim “all nationalities” — the official FAQ restricts eligibility.

Is it really unpaid?

ESA uses the word “unpaid”, but every intern receives a monthly allowance: €800 for non-residents (— or students with special needs) and €500 for residents. Health insurance, accident insurance, travel and accommodation are your own costs.

Can PhD students apply?

No. ESA tells PhD students not to apply for internships and points them to the Research Fellowship programme instead. The internships target master’s-level students, preferably in their final or penultimate year.

How many applications can I submit?

Two per call — and ESA is strict: more than two means all your applications are disregarded. Across your lifetime, you may only ever do one ESA internship.

When does the 2027 call open?

Not yet published. ESA opens the call each November for about a month; the annual pattern points to November 2026 with earliest starts in February 2027. Set a job alert on jobs.esa.int rather than guessing the date.
