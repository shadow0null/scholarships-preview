About the fellowship

The Dragon Flight Fellowship is Tiffin University’s honors experience for students who want to lead in drone and aerial technology. It is structured as a four-year journey that grows with you: Year 1 covers drone operations, safety and regulations; Year 2 builds technical skill and teamwork; Year 3 moves into applied research and leadership in specialized areas; Year 4 is capstone projects, mentorship and a professional launch.

Fellows work on real missions with real partners — public-safety agencies, companies and community organizations — across sectors including environmental monitoring, public safety and emergency response, agriculture, construction and surveying, healthcare logistics, media and filmmaking, and autonomous systems.

What fellows actually get

  • Real flight time and real missions with industry and public-sector partners.
  • Direct FAA engagement — compliance reports, safety initiatives and regulatory reviews.
  • Specialized certifications such as thermal imaging and photogrammetry.
  • Internships with state and local agencies.
  • Research exposure — presenting at conferences or in professional publications.
  • Leadership and mentorship — leading teams and mentoring junior fellows in the final year.

What you do not get, as far as the official page states: a published cash award. Trackers label the programme "funded", but the university’s own page describes an honors experience, not a scholarship cheque. If tuition funding is what you need, look at Tiffin’s separate merit scholarships instead.

Eligibility

  • Enrolment: you must be a Tiffin University student — the programme is open to both online and on-campus undergraduate and graduate students.
  • In-person commitment: weekly in-person participation is required, so online students must be able to attend regularly in Ohio.
  • Academics: the official page publishes no GPA cut-off; scholarship trackers report a 3.0 GPA benchmark (high school for entry, college GPA to continue) — treat this as tracker-reported and confirm with admissions.
  • Nationality: trackers describe it as open to students worldwide; international applicants must additionally meet Tiffin’s admission requirements (including English proficiency).

Documents you will need

  • Tiffin University admission application (undergraduate or graduate, as applicable).
  • Academic transcripts.
  • A short personal essay on why you want to join the fellowship and how drone technology fits your goals.
  • CV/résumé and a valid passport (for international applicants).
  • Any supporting documents the admissions counsellor requests; an interview may follow.

How to apply

  1. Apply for admission to Tiffin University and indicate your interest in fellowship opportunities.
  2. Write the fellowship essay after submitting your application, outlining your interest in the programme.
  3. Speak with your admissions counsellor and complete any interview the university requests.
  4. Meet the 1 February deadline — the official page sets the fellowship application deadline at 1 February each year.
  5. If selected, accept the fellowship terms (active participation in fellowship events and programme regulations) to confirm your place.

FAQs

Is this a scholarship that pays money?

No — not according to the official page. It is an honors fellowship: the value is certifications, FAA and industry experience, internships and mentorship. Do not expect a tuition cheque.

Who can join?

Tiffin University undergraduate and graduate students, on-campus or online — but weekly in-person participation is mandatory, so you need to be reachable for regular on-site sessions.

When is the deadline?

1 February each year, per the official programme page. The next occurrence is 1 February 2027.

Is there a GPA requirement?

The official page does not state one. Scholarship trackers report a 3.0 GPA benchmark for entry and continuation — confirm with the admissions office.

Can international students apply?

Trackers describe the fellowship as open worldwide; you must first meet Tiffin University’s international admission requirements.

What do fellows do day to day?

Hands-on drone operations across sectors — thermal imaging for firefighting, mapping and surveying, FAA compliance work, agency internships, and a final-year capstone with real stakeholders.
