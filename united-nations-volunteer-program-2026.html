Be careful with the label "fully funded" here, because the United Nations Volunteer programme is not one award with one deadline — it is a standing recruitment system with thousands of individual assignments, and what you get depends entirely on which kind of assignment you take. Onsite UN Volunteers serve with a UN host entity — a peace operation, a UN agency, a government partner — and are supported under UNV conditions of service, which include a resettlement allowance on successful completion. Online Volunteers, by contrast, work remotely on defined tasks, receive no allowance or benefits at all, and are capped at 20 hours a week for a maximum of 12 weeks. Both routes run through the same platform, the UNV Unified Volunteering Platform, and both are open year-round: each vacancy carries its own closing date, so there is no single programme deadline to miss.

About the programme

United Nations Volunteers is the UN organisation that deploys volunteers to support peace, development and humanitarian work. It runs two distinct routes. Onsite UN Volunteers take up full-time assignments — typically six to twelve months, sometimes longer — inside a host UN entity, in duty stations around the world, including hardship locations. They work as part of the host team, not as interns, and the assignments span everything from child protection and public health to programme management, communications, logistics and data work. Online Volunteering is the remote counterpart: volunteers anywhere in the world take on defined tasks — writing, research, translation, graphic design, digital marketing — for host organisations working towards the Sustainable Development Goals, without travelling and without a contract with UNV or the engaging organisation.

That distinction drives everything else in this article. If a listing promises a "fully funded UN Volunteer scholarship" with a single deadline, it is almost certainly describing onsite assignments in general terms, or it is simply wrong. Read the individual vacancy: it states the duty station, the duration, the required experience and languages, and the closing date.

Benefits

  • Onsite assignments: support under UNV conditions of service, set out in each vacancy's description of assignment. Summaries of the programme list a resettlement allowance paid after successful completion of the assignment, alongside the living and assignment support that comes with serving in a duty station. Amounts vary by assignment and duty station, so no single figure is stated here.
  • Online volunteering: no allowance, financial compensation or benefits — this is unpaid, remote, task-based volunteering. The return is experience, skills, a certificate of completion for the assignment, and a stronger profile for future UN or development roles.
  • Both routes: practical experience inside the UN system, professional networks, and work that counts as real UN experience on a CV. UNV charges no recruitment fee at any stage — any request for payment connected to a UNV application is a scam signal.

Who can apply

  • Age: at least 18 years old for both routes. Some youth-focused onsite programmes set their own upper age limits — the vacancy states them.
  • Onsite roles: requirements depend on the specific role. Professional specialist roles typically require a degree and relevant work experience (for example, a recent UNICEF specialist vacancy asked for a Bachelor's degree in social work, psychology or a related field plus at least three years of relevant experience); community-level roles set lower bars. Language requirements are stated per vacancy — working knowledge of English, French or Spanish is commonly required.
  • Online volunteering: open to adults aged 18 or over worldwide. There is no single academic or professional background for the whole service; each host organisation sets the skills, language and experience requirements for its own task. No IELTS or TOEFL is required as a blanket rule.
  • Commitment: online assignments run up to 20 hours a week for a maximum of 12 weeks; onsite assignments are full-time for their stated duration.

Documents required

  • A UNV profile on the Unified Volunteering Platform (app.unv.org) — education, skills, languages and professional experience. This profile is your application base; there is no separate paper form.
  • Vacancy-specific material as each assignment asks for it — typically a motivation statement tailored to the role, and sometimes work samples.
  • Online volunteering: register on the platform, complete your profile, and apply to advertised tasks that match your skills. Host organisations set the task requirements and the closing date for each listing.

How to apply

  1. Create an account on the UNV Unified Volunteering Platform (UVP) at app.unv.org and build a complete profile — education, skills, languages and experience.
  2. Search the advertised assignments (onsite) or the online assignment board (remote) and read each vacancy in full: duty station, duration, requirements and closing date.
  3. Apply directly through the platform to the roles that match your profile. For onsite roles, only applications submitted through the official process are considered.
  4. Apply early for any vacancy with a near closing date — for example, specialist onsite vacancies have closed within days of advertisement — and never pay anyone who claims to secure a UNV placement.

FAQs

What is the deadline for the United Nations Volunteer Program 2026?

There is no single deadline. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis throughout the year, and each advertised assignment carries its own closing date — check the vacancy, not a roundup page.

Is the UN Volunteer Program fully funded?

Only in the limited sense that onsite assignments are supported under UNV conditions of service, including a resettlement allowance on successful completion. Online volunteering is unpaid, with no allowance or benefits. Neither route is a scholarship with a fixed cash package.

Is UN Online Volunteering paid?

No. Online Volunteers receive no living allowance, financial compensation or benefits, and have no contract with UNV or the engaging organisation. Assignments run up to 20 hours a week for a maximum of 12 weeks.

Who can apply for UN Volunteering?

Anyone aged 18 or over. Onsite professional roles typically need a degree and relevant experience; online volunteering has no single academic bar — each host organisation states its own requirements.

Do I need IELTS to volunteer with the UN?

No blanket IELTS or TOEFL requirement applies. Vacancies state their own language requirements, commonly working knowledge of English, French or Spanish.

Does UNV charge an application fee?

No. UNV charges no recruitment fee, and the United Nations does not charge any application, processing, training, interview, testing or recruitment fee. Treat any payment request as a scam.
