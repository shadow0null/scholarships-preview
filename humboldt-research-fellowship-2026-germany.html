Do not apply today, and do not trust the deadline lists that say you can. The Alexander von Humboldt Foundation, one of Germany most respected research funders, has capped its current Humboldt Research Fellowship call at 800 applications, and that cap has been reached: no new applications can be created at present. The next call opens on 15 November 2026, for the selection meeting in July 2027, with the earliest fellowship start in September 2027. That single fact changes how this opportunity should be read. It is not a rolling open door, and it is not a fixed annual deadline in March, July and November, as aggregator pages claim. It is a competitive, capped call that you prepare for now and submit when it opens. The award itself is substantial: EUR 3,000 a month for postdoctoral researchers and EUR 3,600 a month for experienced researchers, plus language training, family benefits, a health-insurance subsidy, travel allowances and a subsidy for the host research costs. If you are a researcher with a strong record, a German host, and a project that needs time in Germany, the wait until 15 November is preparation time, not dead time.

About the fellowship

The Humboldt Research Fellowship is a fellowship for researchers, not a scholarship for students. It funds a research stay at a university or research institution in Germany, hosted by an established German academic who mentors the fellow and provides research facilities. The Foundation, based in Bonn, has run the programme for decades, and its alumni network is one of the reasons the award carries weight far beyond the monthly stipend. Selection is competitive, with an expected approval rate of 20 to 25 percent, and the process takes months: applications are reviewed formally, expert reviews are collected, a selection committee meets, and the outcome arrives about five weeks after the selection decision. Applicants should therefore plan on a four to eight month process from submission to decision, and should not build a move to Germany around a start date the Foundation has not confirmed.

The cap is the newest feature, and it is the reason this article leads with a warning rather than a deadline. The official page states that the current call has reached its maximum of 800 applications and that no new applications can be created at present. Older aggregator pages, which list three deadlines a year in March, July and November, are now stale: they describe the old rhythm, not the capped call. The next call opens on 15 November 2026. The practical consequence is simple. Use the weeks before 15 November to secure your host, your mentoring agreement, your research outline and your two expert reviewers, so that you can submit promptly when the call opens, rather than starting that work after it opens and risking a second cap.

Benefits

  • EUR 3,000 a month for postdoctoral researchers, EUR 3,600 a month for experienced researchers: these are the current official rates. Older aggregator figures of EUR 2,670, EUR 2,700 and EUR 3,200 are outdated and should not be used. The fellowship is a living stipend, not a salary, and it is paid for the duration of the stay.
  • Duration with flexibility: postdoctoral fellowships run 6 to 24 months, experienced-researcher fellowships 6 to 18 months. Either can be divided into up to three stays within three years, which matters for researchers who cannot relocate for one continuous block.
  • Language training: an intensive language course for the fellow and partner before the fellowship begins, so that daily life in Germany does not depend on arriving fluent.
  • Family benefits and health-insurance subsidy: family benefits where applicable, and a subsidy for private full health insurance, both of which change the real value of the stipend for researchers moving with a partner or children.
  • Travel allowances and host research costs: travel allowances for the fellow, and a subsidy to the host for research costs, so that the host institution is supported as well as the fellow.
  • Personal support and the Humboldt network: personal support during the fellowship and, afterwards, membership of a worldwide alumni network that remains a professional asset long after the stay ends.

Who can apply

  • Nationality: researchers of all nationalities except Germany, with narrow exceptions for German nationals who have lived and worked abroad for a long time. If you are German and based in Germany, this fellowship is not for you; the Foundation directs alumni and other routes to different programmes.
  • Postdoctoral track: a doctorate or equivalent completed within the last four years, or to be completed before the envisaged selection meeting, with a written summary of findings. Aggregator pages that invert this rule, saying the doctorate must be at least four years old, are wrong.
  • Experienced-researcher track: a doctorate within the last twelve years, an extensive publication list including lead-author papers, and a leadership-level position. An alternative route exists for researchers up to sixteen years after a master or postgraduate degree where a doctorate is not possible or uncommon, with a commensurate publication record.
  • German host required: you must have a host at a German research institution who provides a mentoring agreement, confirmation of research facilities, and a detailed review of your project. An application without a committed host is not a complete application.
  • Two expert reviews: two independent expert reviews, uploaded directly by the reviewers, are required. Line up reviewers early: a late review is a common reason for delay.
  • Residence rule: you must not have lived in Germany for more than 90 days in total in the eighteen months before applying. Good German or English is required, depending on the field, and previous direct Humboldt sponsorship disqualifies an applicant from this fellowship.

Documents required

  • Curriculum vitae, maximum two pages, and a research outline of maximum five pages. Brevity is a requirement here, not a suggestion: overlong documents are a formal weakness.
  • Complete publication list, a list of selected key publications, and the key publications themselves, so that reviewers can judge the record, not just the claim.
  • Doctoral certificate, or proof of successful completion, or confirmation that completion will occur before the selection meeting, plus a German language certificate if required, and publisher acceptance notifications or translations where required.
  • Host statement and confirmation of research facilities, with the detailed host review, and the two expert reviews uploaded directly by the reviewers.
  • Online submission only: applications go online to the Foundation. Incomplete or inaccurate applications are formally rejected or postponed, so check every field and attachment before submitting.

How to apply

  1. Secure a German host first. Agree the project, the mentoring arrangement and the research facilities, and confirm that the host will provide the detailed statement and review.
  2. Prepare the research outline (maximum five pages), the two-page CV, the publication lists and the key publications, and the doctoral evidence. Keep to the page limits exactly.
  3. Ask your two expert reviewers early, and confirm they will upload their reviews directly. Do not leave this to the week the call opens.
  4. When the call opens on 15 November 2026, submit online to the Foundation, complete and accurate, for the July 2027 selection meeting. Expect a decision about five weeks after that meeting, with the earliest start in September 2027.
  5. Do not confuse this fellowship with the separate CAPES-Humboldt programme for Brazil, which has its own rules and deadlines. It is a different scheme.

FAQs

Can I apply right now?

No. The current call has reached its 800-application cap and no new applications can be created at present. The next call opens 15 November 2026, for the July 2027 selection meeting, with the earliest start in September 2027.

How much does the fellowship pay?

EUR 3,000 a month for postdoctoral researchers and EUR 3,600 a month for experienced researchers, plus language training, family benefits, a health-insurance subsidy, travel allowances and a host research-cost subsidy. Older figures of EUR 2,670 or EUR 2,700 are outdated.

How long can the fellowship last?

Postdoctoral fellowships 6 to 24 months, experienced-researcher fellowships 6 to 18 months, each divisible into up to three stays within three years.

Do I need a German host before applying?

Yes. A German host providing a mentoring agreement, confirmation of research facilities and a detailed review is required, along with two expert reviews.

Am I eligible if I have lived in Germany recently?

Only if your total stay in Germany in the eighteen months before applying is 90 days or less. Longer residence disqualifies you under the residence rule.

What are my chances?

The Foundation expects an approval rate of 20 to 25 percent. A strong publication record, a clear research outline and a committed host are the factors you control.
