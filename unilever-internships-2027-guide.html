About the internship programme

Unilever does not run one global summer internship. What it runs is a per-market early-careers system: marketing, supply chain, technology, finance, R&D, human resources and customer-development internships are posted as individual job listings for individual countries on careers.unilever.com. Each listing carries its own location, function, duration, eligibility and start date — and applications are reviewed on a rolling basis, meaning a role closes as soon as Unilever has enough strong applicants. There is no published master deadline to work toward; the working rule is apply as soon as a role matching your profile appears.

The most structured tracks are the Unilever Leadership Internship Programme (ULIP) in markets like Singapore and the Industrial Placements in the UK & Ireland — year-long placements embedded in degree programmes.

What the internship offers

  • Duration: varies by market and role — about 6 months for Singapore ULIP, 12 months for UK Industrial Placements, and shorter summer slots in markets like Belgium.
  • Real projects: ULIP Singapore projects include route-to-market and partner-onboarding work; UK placements sit in functions from technology to supply chain.
  • Pay: not published on the official posting pages reviewed for this article. Third-party guides describe the programme as paid, and one cites £27,898 per annum for a UK Technology Industrial Placement 2027 (job ref R-1188417) — treat that as reported, not officially confirmed.
  • Conversion: internship performance feeds into graduate hiring, though no official conversion guarantee is published — do not treat the internship as a job offer.
  • Time commitment: Singapore ULIP asks for a minimum of 3 days a week, with full-time preferred; UK placements are full-time.

Eligibility

  • Enrolled students, not fresh graduates: Singapore ULIP explicitly requires applicants to be enrolled in a bachelor’s or master’s degree programme — fresh graduates are ineligible. UK Industrial Placements require students to have completed their penultimate year and be returning to university after the placement.
  • Regions are per-listing: the Singapore posting is for candidates able to work in Singapore; UK roles sit at sites like Port Sunlight, Leeds, Burton and Kingston and expect relocation. Aggregator claims of “all nationalities welcome” are not supported by the official postings.
  • Functions differ per market: Customer Development, Human Resources, Consumer Marketing / Digital Marketing, Supply Chain, Technology, Finance and R&D appear across the live postings — check each listing.
  • One application per market: the UK page asks applicants to apply for only one function — only the first application is considered.

Documents you will need

Each posting lists its own requirements, but across the official pages the consistent asks are:

  • A CV — always.
  • Academic transcripts and certificates for some roles (e.g. finance internships).
  • Evidence of your right to work in the posting’s country, where applicable.

Check the specific posting’s “What we need” section rather than relying on a generic checklist.

How to apply

  1. Go to careers.unilever.com and search for internship / early-careers roles, filtering by country and function.
  2. Open a specific posting (for example, the Singapore ULIP Customer Development role, ref R-1187705) and read the whole listing — eligibility, dates and process are listing-specific.
  3. Create an account and click “Apply Online” — early. Rolling review means the best roles can fill weeks before any nominal closing date.
  4. Complete the assessments. The Singapore ULIP process runs: online application → SOVA blended assessments → phone interview → virtual interview with the hiring manager → offer.
  5. Respect the one-function rule on the UK & Ireland hub — apply to one function only, or only your first application counts.

For the US Summer Intern Program, third-party guides expect postings on Unilever’s Workday portal between September and November 2026 for summer 2027 — as of early October 2026 no US intern postings were live, so verify directly on careers.unilever.com.

Frequently asked questions

Is there one global Unilever Summer Internship application?

No. Unilever posts internships as individual country- and function-specific roles on careers.unilever.com. There is no single programme, no single deadline, and no single eligibility list — every listing stands alone.

Are summer 2026 internships still open?

No. The summer 2026 postings found (e.g. Belgium digital marketing starting July–September 2026, Singapore ULIP roles running 13 July–31 December 2026) have passed. The open intakes are for 2027.

Can fresh graduates apply?

For the flagship tracks, no. Singapore ULIP explicitly requires current enrolment and lists fresh graduates as ineligible; UK Industrial Placements require penultimate-year students returning to their degree.

Is the internship paid?

Unilever’s own posting pages do not publish pay figures. Third-party sources describe the programme as paid — one cites £27,898 per annum for a UK Technology placement — but that is reported, not officially confirmed.

What is the selection process?

Typically: online application, SOVA blended assessments, phone interview, then a virtual interview with the hiring manager. Steps vary by market — read the posting.

How do deadlines work with rolling review?

There are no fixed deadlines on most postings. Roles are reviewed as applications arrive and close once filled, so applying early materially improves your chances.
