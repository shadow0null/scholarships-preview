Read the funding label with care, because aggregator pages routinely call this opportunity fully funded. It is not a full-funding package. The International Fund for Agricultural Development, a United Nations agency fighting rural poverty and hunger, runs a rolling internship programme of six months, with a monthly allowance and, for interns from developing countries who must travel to their duty station, a higher allowance towards housing and travel. There is no single closing date: you apply to a specific vacancy on the IFAD careers portal, and vacancies are filled as they arise. The exact allowance amount is not published on the official programme page, so this article states none. If you are 30 or under, studying or recently graduated in a field that touches IFAD work, and you can work in Rome or in a country or regional office, this is a genuine route into the United Nations system. If you need every cost covered in advance, check the vacancy terms before you apply, because the official page promises an allowance, not full funding.

About the programme

IFAD invests in rural people. Its mandate is to help poor rural communities in developing countries raise their incomes and food security, and its internship programme is built to bring students and recent graduates into that work, rather than to run a classroom course. Interns are placed where the work actually sits: at headquarters in Rome, or in a country or regional office around the world. That placement matters more than the city in the queue title. The title says Italy, because headquarters is in Rome, but a vacancy in a country office is just as much an IFAD internship, and applicants should search the whole vacancy list rather than waiting for a Rome posting.

The programme is deliberately open-ended. There is no annual competition with one deadline and one intake. Vacancies appear through the year, each with its own description, duty station and requirements, and each is filled when a suitable candidate is found. That is why this article gives no closing date and no batch calendar: inventing one would mislead readers into waiting for, or missing, a real vacancy. The practical rule is the opposite of a fixed-deadline scholarship. Check the portal regularly, and apply when a vacancy matches your field and your availability, because a good vacancy can close once it is filled.

Benefits

  • A six-month placement, extendable once: the standard internship lasts six months, and the official programme page allows one additional six-month internship in a different area. It is a substantial placement, not a short visit, and it is long enough to complete a real piece of work.
  • A monthly allowance: interns receive a monthly allowance from IFAD. The official programme page does not publish the amount, so this article does not quote one. Any page that gives you a precise figure for this programme is quoting an aggregator or an old vacancy, not the current official page, and you should confirm the figure in the vacancy itself before relying on it.
  • A higher allowance where travel is required: interns from developing countries who must travel to their duty station receive a higher monthly allowance, contributing to housing and travel costs. This is the programme's main equalising feature, and it is worth checking in the vacancy terms if you would need to relocate.
  • United Nations experience in rural development: work in areas such as agriculture, rural finance, climate adaptation, nutrition, gender, or project design, depending on the host team. That experience, and the references that come with it, carry weight for later United Nations, development-bank and non-governmental roles.
  • What it does not promise: the official page does not promise full funding of all costs, a salary, or a job at the end. Do not budget as if the allowance covered everything, and do not treat selection as a route to employment: the internship is a learning placement, and any further role would need a separate application.

Who can apply

  • Age 30 or younger: the official programme page sets an upper age limit of 30. If you are over 30, this programme is closed to you, however strong your profile.
  • Enrolled, or recently finished: you must be enrolled in an accredited university or graduate school, having completed at least two years of undergraduate study and attended courses in the last 24 months, or you must have completed undergraduate or postgraduate studies within the last 24 months. Aggregator pages that say 12 months are wrong: the official window is 24 months.
  • English fluency: fluency in English is required. Knowledge of another IFAD official language, Arabic, French or Spanish, is an asset, and for some country-office posts it may be required by the vacancy itself. Check the language line in each vacancy, not just the programme page.
  • A field relevant to IFAD work: your studies or experience should connect to IFAD's mission, whether that is agriculture, economics, development studies, environment, finance, communications, or a related discipline. A vacancy in a technical team will also carry its own subject requirements.
  • Available for the full placement: internships are full-time placements at a duty station or, where the vacancy says so, in a hybrid arrangement. Confirm the working pattern in the vacancy before applying, and do not assume a fully remote option exists.

Documents required

  • The official programme page does not publish a document checklist, so this article does not invent one. Aggregator lists of a CV, motivation letter, transcripts and proof of enrolment or graduation are plausible portal requirements, but they are not confirmed on the official programme page and they will vary by vacancy.
  • Prepare the standard set in advance: an up-to-date CV, a motivation letter tailored to the specific vacancy and team, academic transcripts, and proof of enrolment or of recent graduation. Having these ready matters because vacancies fill as they arise.
  • Tailor, do not recycle: because you apply to a specific team, your motivation letter should name the work that team does and the skills you bring to it. A generic United Nations letter is the weakest application this programme receives.
  • No application fee is charged: consistent with United Nations practice, secondary sources confirm no fee. Any page asking for payment to apply for an IFAD internship is not IFAD.

How to apply

  1. Go to the IFAD careers portal and search for internship vacancies, filtering by the programme name. Do not apply through an aggregator link that promises a generic form: there is no single generic application form.
  2. Read the vacancy in full: duty station, team, duration, language requirements and any allowance or travel terms stated for that post. If a term you need is not stated, treat it as unconfirmed rather than assuming it.
  3. Create a profile on the portal, complete the application for that specific vacancy, and upload the documents the portal asks for. Keep a copy of everything you submit.
  4. Apply as soon as a suitable vacancy appears, rather than waiting for a deadline that does not exist. Rolling programmes reward early, well-matched applications.
  5. If you are shortlisted, expect the host team to assess fit for its own work. Prepare to discuss, concretely, what you would do in the first weeks of the placement.

FAQs

Is there a deadline for the IFAD Internship Programme?

No single deadline is published. The programme is rolling: you apply to a specific vacancy on the IFAD careers portal, and vacancies are filled as they arise. Check the portal regularly and apply when a vacancy matches you.

How much is the monthly allowance?

The exact amount is not published on the official programme page, so this article states none. Interns from developing countries who must travel to their duty station receive a higher allowance towards housing and travel. Confirm the figure in the vacancy itself before relying on any number quoted elsewhere.

Is the internship only in Rome?

No. Headquarters is in Rome, but internships also sit in IFAD country and regional offices worldwide. Search the whole vacancy list, not only Rome postings.

Am I eligible if I graduated more than two years ago?

No, on the official rule. You must be enrolled with courses attended in the last 24 months, or have completed undergraduate or postgraduate studies within the last 24 months. Aggregator pages saying 12 months are wrong.

How long does the internship last?

Six months, with the possibility of one additional six-month internship in a different area.

Is this a paid job or a route to IFAD employment?

Neither, in the strict sense. It is an internship with a monthly allowance, not a salary and not a job offer. Any later role would need a separate application.
