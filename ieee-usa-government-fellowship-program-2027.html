About the fellowship

IEEE-USA, the U.S. arm of the world’s largest technical professional organisation, runs its Government Fellowship Program to place engineers and technologists inside the federal government for a year. There are three tracks: the Congressional Fellowship, working on Capitol Hill; the Engineering & Diplomacy Fellowship, at the U.S. Department of State; and the Congressional Electric Grid Policy Fellowship, focused on grid policy. Fellows spend the year advising on legislation and policy, bringing a technical perspective to decisions that are usually made without one — and the experience routinely shapes careers in public policy, industry and academia.

The 2027–28 fellowship year runs 1 September 2027 to 31 August 2028, and the application deadline is 1 January 2027, per the official IEEE-USA page (updated 13 October 2026). The award is reported by aggregators at about $100,000 in total — roughly a $95,000 stipend plus a $5,000 relocation allowance — but that figure does not appear on the official IEEE-USA page, so this article presents it as aggregator-reported. Finalists are interviewed in person in Washington, D.C. on 9–10 February 2027, and those dates cannot be rescheduled.

Eligibility

  • Be a U.S. citizen — mandatory, stated explicitly on the official page. This fellowship is not open to international applicants.
  • Be an active IEEE member in good standing (graduate student members are eligible). The Electric Grid Policy track does not require membership at the application stage but requires joining if selected.
  • Hold a PhD, or a Master’s degree plus at least five years of full-time relevant professional experience (aggregator-sourced; not stated on the official page section read).
  • Have a strong science, engineering or technology background, excellent written and verbal communication, and a demonstrated interest in public policy.
  • Be able to relocate to Washington, D.C. for the full year.

Benefits

  • About $100,000 in total (aggregator-reported: ~$95,000 stipend + $5,000 relocation allowance) — not confirmed on the official page.
  • A year working on Capitol Hill (Congressional Fellowship), at the U.S. Department of State (Engineering & Diplomacy Fellowship), or on Electric Grid Policy.
  • Direct experience of legislation and policy-making from inside government.
  • Fellowship year: 1 September 2027 – 31 August 2028.

Documents to prepare

  • A letter of intent.
  • A personal/technical resume.
  • Three letters of reference.

How to apply

  1. Open the official IEEE-USA fellowships page and choose your track (Congressional, Engineering & Diplomacy, or Electric Grid Policy).
  2. Apply directly to IEEE-USA through the “Apply Online” links on that page — do not apply via AAAS.
  3. Submit your letter of intent, resume and three letters of reference by 1 January 2027.
  4. If selected as a finalist, attend the in-person interview in Washington, D.C. on 9–10 February 2027 — interviews cannot be rescheduled.

1 January 2027 for the 2027–28 fellowships, per the official IEEE-USA page (updated 13 October 2026). Two aggregators give 8 January 2027 — use the official 1 January date.

How much is the IEEE-USA Government Fellowship worth?
