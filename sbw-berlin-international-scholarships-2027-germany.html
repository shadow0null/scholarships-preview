About the scholarship

The SBW Berlin Scholarship is run by SBW Berlin gemeinnützige GmbH, a non-profit that supports talented young people from countries in particular need of development support to study in Berlin — and then to take what they learned back home. It is fully funded: a furnished room in a shared student flat in Berlin, a living allowance of €480 a month and tuition support, for up to 48 months (the regular programme duration), plus in some cases a travel contribution to and from Germany. There are only about 20 places in total, awarded as vacancies arise — which is why there is no fixed deadline and applications are accepted year-round.

What makes SBW Berlin different from an ordinary scholarship is the deal attached to it. You are not just funded; you are expected to run a social project in your home country — 2 to 8 hours a week with a non-profit — for the whole time you are funded, to attend SBW Berlin project meetings and workshops, and to report and present on your project. After graduation you are expected to work at least 18 months in your home country or engage in charitable activities in Germany until the repayment plan is fulfilled. And if you discontinue your studies or your project, or you stay in Germany, a repayment plan starts three months later, in 12 monthly instalments. Apply only if that deal suits you — and apply early, because university admission, uni-assist, language testing and the visa take months even without a deadline to chase.

Eligibility

  • Be a foreign national from a country in particular need of development support (see the SBW Berlin guidelines).
  • Be aged 18–30.
  • Have professional or volunteer experience in the non-profit or social sector, supported by letters of recommendation from the social institution.
  • Not have resided in Germany for more than 18 months before applying.
  • Hold a university entrance qualification, with an average grade equivalent to a German 2.0 or better.
  • Have a proven relatively low net income.
  • Have no first-degree family members permanently residing in Germany.
  • Apply before starting studies or while enrolled in the 1st, 2nd or at most 3rd semester at a state-recognised university — or for a master’s shortly before or after a bachelor’s.
  • Intend to work at least 18 months in your home country after graduation, or engage in charitable activities in Germany until the repayment plan is fulfilled.
  • Apply separately to a Berlin or Potsdam university — the scholarship does not include admission.

Benefits

  • A furnished room in a shared student apartment in Berlin.
  • A living allowance of €480 a month.
  • Tuition support (subject to the scholarship rules).
  • In some cases, a travel contribution to and from Germany.
  • Funding for up to 48 months, reviewed periodically.
  • Not included: health insurance — you arrange and pay mandatory public health insurance yourself (about €120–140 a month, per aggregators).

Documents to prepare

  • A valid passport.
  • A German student visa.
  • Proof of admission to a Berlin or Potsdam university.
  • Academic certificates and transcripts.
  • A CV.
  • A language proficiency certificate.
  • A letter of recommendation from a previous tutor or academic supervisor.
  • A description of a social or development project.
  • Proof of financial need (bank statement).
  • Evidence of previous work or professional experience.
  • Note that this list is aggregator-sourced, not officially confirmed — the official page does not list the documents in full, so check the guidelines on the SBW Berlin site.

How to apply

  1. Go to the official page, sbw.berlin/en/sbw-berlin-scholarship/, and read the guidelines linked there.
  2. Secure the recommendation letter from your non-profit or social institution first — the application is two-step (per aggregators).
  3. Apply separately for admission to a Berlin or Potsdam university.
  4. Submit the SBW Berlin application with your documents — there is no application fee and no fixed deadline.
  5. Apply early: admission, uni-assist, language testing and the visa take months.

FAQs

What is the deadline for the SBW Berlin Scholarship 2027?

There is no fixed deadline. Applications are accepted year-round and places are awarded as vacancies arise. The “31 December 2026” dates on some aggregator sites are not official — do not plan around them.

How much does the SBW Berlin Scholarship pay?

A furnished room in a shared student flat in Berlin, €480 a month in living allowance and tuition support, for up to 48 months, plus in some cases a travel contribution. Health insurance is not included.

Who can apply for the SBW Berlin Scholarship?

Foreign nationals aged 18–30 from countries in particular need of development support, with non-profit or social-sector experience, a relatively low income and a German grade of 2.0 or better — who have not lived in Germany for more than 18 months and who apply before or early in their studies at a Berlin or Potsdam university.

What is the social project requirement?

Recipients must run a social project (2–8 hours a week) with a non-profit in their home country during their studies, attend SBW Berlin project meetings and workshops, write project reports and give presentations.

Do I have to pay the money back?

Only if you discontinue your studies or your project, or you stay in Germany after graduation. Then a repayment plan starts three months after the end of studies or funding, in 12 monthly instalments.

Is health insurance covered?

No — on the aggregator evidence, health insurance is not included. You arrange and pay mandatory public health insurance yourself (about €120–140 a month). One aggregator claims otherwise, but that claim conflicts with the others and is not used here.
